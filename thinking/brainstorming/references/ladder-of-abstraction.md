# Ladder of Abstraction — from thinking/ladder-of-abstraction

> Move text between concrete (sensory) and abstract (philosophical).

## Core Principle
From linguist S.I. Hayakawa:
- **Bottom of ladder**: most concrete, pure sensory experience
- **Top of ladder**: most abstract, conceptual, pithy
- **Middle**: mix of abstraction and detail

## When to Use
- Writing feels vague — make it concrete
- Writing feels too detailed — make it abstract
- Concept clarification
- Speech refinement

## Method

### Move DOWN (make concrete)
- Drop into sensory experience
- Focus on detail, zoom in
- Create atmosphere without stating mood
- Use specific, vivid language

### Move UP (make abstract)
- Extract the essential concept
- Compress and simplify
- Use philosophical language
- One-sentence clarity

## Examples

**To concrete**: "The rider was very hungry, ate quickly"
→ "A moment later, the bowl was empty. The rider lifted it to his mouth, throat working, a sound filling the room."

**To abstract**: "Mountain is a浪 of geological time"
→ "Solid-state fluid dynamics characterization at geological timescales"
